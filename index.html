Privacy Policy

EchoRoutine

Effective date: 7 November 2026 · replaces the version dated 6 November 2026

EchoRoutine ("the app") is a voice-guided routine, checklist and timer app for Android, for anyone who wants a spoken, hands-free daily schedule with workout timers and water, food, weight and sleep tracking; its clock also understands shift work, so a day can run past midnight. It is published by an independent developer (Kevin, github.com/darkskys106). This policy explains what data the app handles, the few cases where anything leaves your phone, and how to contact the developer.

Summary

Everything you enter is stored on your own device. The developer does not collect, receive, store or have access to any of it, and there are no accounts, no advertising and no tracking. Data leaves your phone only in the situations listed under "When data leaves your phone": a barcode lookup you trigger, anonymous diagnostics from Google's barcode-scanning library, an AI request you set up with your own API key, backups you choose to make, and a problem report or suggestion you choose to email to the developer.

Data stored on your device

The app keeps the following in its private storage on your phone, where other apps cannot read it:

  • Your routines and routine profiles (the items, times, types and notes you create or import)
  • Checklist completion state for each day, which also drives the streak and "last 7 days" summaries
  • Workout timer sets and rep records
  • Food log entries, custom foods, nutrition targets and water intake
  • Body-weight entries, sleep ratings and, if you log them, your lights-out and wake-up times
  • Custom timers and your settings (voice, notification, backup and timer preferences)
  • If you connect an AI provider: the API key you entered, stored encrypted using Android's hardware-backed keystore

None of this is transmitted to the developer. It stays on the device until you delete it (see "Your choices and deleting data").

When data leaves your phone

1. Barcode lookup (Open Food Facts)

When you scan a food barcode, the app sends the barcode number to the public Open Food Facts database to fetch the product's name, brand and nutrition values. The request contains the barcode number and the app's name and version (its "user agent"); like any internet request, the server also sees your device's IP address. Nothing about you or your logs is sent. This only happens when you actively scan, and if the lookup fails you can enter the food manually. Open Food Facts is run by a non-profit that is not connected to the developer; see their privacy policy.

2. Barcode recognition (Google ML Kit)

Barcodes are recognised on the device by Google's ML Kit barcode-scanning library. Camera images are processed locally and are never uploaded or stored. Google states that the library sends it anonymous diagnostics and usage statistics over an encrypted connection: device information (manufacturer, model, OS version), the app's package name and version, a per-installation identifier that Google says is not intended to identify a user or device, performance metrics, event types and error codes. Google says it does not pass this data to third parties. Details are in Google's ML Kit data disclosure. This library only runs while the barcode scanner screen is open.

3. Optional AI routine generation (bring your own key)

EchoRoutine can turn a typed description of your week into a routine, in two ways:

  • Copy a prompt, paste the reply. The app shows you a prompt to copy into any AI chat app or website you already use, and you paste the reply back. The app itself sends nothing anywhere for this option.
  • Connect your own API key. If you enter an API key for Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI (ChatGPT) or Google (Gemini) in Settings, the app sends your request, together with the app's instructions and your key, directly to that provider's API over HTTPS. Depending on what you ask for, the request contains the description or change you typed, your current routine (when you ask for a change to it), the name and brand of a food (when you ask for a macro estimate), or a one-line test message (when you save the key). Your key is stored only on your device and is sent only to the provider it belongs to — never to the developer. Those requests are governed by the provider's own policy: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google. You can change or remove the key at any time in Settings, which stops this feature until you add one again.

4. Backups you set up

  • Export and import. You can export your data as a JSON file to a location you choose and import such a file later. Files are created and read only when you ask.
  • Automatic backup. If you pick a backup folder in Settings, the app keeps three backup files there (echoroutine-auto-1, -2 and -3): one refreshed daily when something changed, one about a week old and one about a month old. If the folder you pick is synced by another app (for example a cloud-drive folder), that app's terms apply to the copies it makes. You can turn this off or change the folder at any time.
  • Android device backup. Android's own backup service may include EchoRoutine's data in your device backup to your Google account, encrypted, as it does for other apps. You control this in your phone's Settings under Google › Backup.

5. Problem reports and suggestions (email)

Settings › About has Report a problem and Send a suggestion buttons. They open your own email app with a draft addressed to darkskys106@gmail.com. The draft is pre-filled with the app version and build number, your Android version and your phone's make and model, because those are usually needed to understand a problem; you can edit or delete any of it before sending, and nothing is sent until you press Send in your email app. The app has no access to your email account or messages. Emails you send arrive in the developer's Gmail account (run by Google), are kept only to reply to you and to fix the problem, and are not shared with anyone else.

Purchases

EchoRoutine is sold through Google Play, and the Settings screen offers optional tips as in-app purchases. Tips unlock nothing; they are a way to say thanks. Google processes every payment and holds your payment details; the developer never sees them and only receives aggregated sales reporting from Google. Google's handling of your purchase is described in Google's privacy policy.

What the app does not do

  • No user accounts, sign-in or registration
  • No advertising, advertising identifiers or ad tracking
  • No analytics or usage tracking by the developer (the only diagnostics are the ML Kit ones described above)
  • No selling of data and no sharing for marketing purposes
  • No access to your contacts, location, microphone, call logs, email account, or files beyond the backup locations you pick (the report buttons only hand a draft to your email app)

Your choices and deleting data

  • Delete individual routines, entries or logs inside the app at any time.
  • Settings › Reset all app data wipes everything the app has stored on the device, including any API key.
  • Uninstalling the app removes its private storage. Backup files you exported stay where you put them until you delete them.
  • Apart from emails you choose to send (section 5 above), the developer never receives your data, so there is nothing held on the developer's side to access, correct or delete. To have an email you sent deleted, reply to it and ask. If you have a question about data held by Google (Play purchases, ML Kit diagnostics, Android backup) or by an AI provider you connected, their policies above explain how to contact them.

Children

The app is not directed at children and is not intended for use by anyone under 13. It does not knowingly collect data from children.
